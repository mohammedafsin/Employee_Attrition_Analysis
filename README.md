After analysing the employee attrition dataset, I found that out of 1,470 employees, 237 employees have left the organisation, resulting in an overall attrition rate of 16.12%. The organisation currently has 1,233 active employees.

The major findings are:

1. Department-wise attrition: The Sales department has the highest attrition rate at 20.63%, followed by Human Resources at 19.05%.

2. Job role-wise attrition: Sales Representatives have the highest attrition rate at 39.76%. Laboratory Technicians and HR employees also show comparatively high attrition.

3. Overtime: Employees working overtime have a much higher attrition rate (30.53%) compared to employees who do not work overtime (10.44%).

4. Age group: Younger employees aged 18–25 have the highest attrition rate of 35.77%. This indicates that employee retention among younger workers needs more attention.

5. Gender: Male employees have a slightly higher attrition rate (17.01%) than female employees (14.80%).

6. Marital status: Single employees have the highest attrition rate at 25.53%, compared with married and divorced employees.

7. Work-life balance: Employees with the lowest work-life balance rating have a higher attrition rate of 31.25%.

Overall, the analysis shows that overtime, job roles, age groups and work-life balance are important areas for the organisation to focus on when improving employee retention.

11. Business Recommendations to Reduce Employee Attrition

Based on the analysis, I would recommend the following actions to the organisation:

1. Improve employee satisfaction: The organisation should regularly collect employee feedback and understand their workplace problems.

2. Reduce overtime: Management should distribute workloads properly and avoid putting excessive pressure on employees.

3. Provide career opportunities: Training, promotions and career development programmes can encourage employees to continue working with the organisation.

4. Focus on the Sales department: The organisation should review sales targets, provide suitable incentives and recognise employee performance.

5. Support younger employees: Mentoring programmes and skill development opportunities can help young employees build their careers.

6. Improve work-life balance: Flexible working arrangements, proper workload planning and employee wellness activities can improve employee satisfaction.

7. Recognise employee performance: Rewards, appreciation and recognition can motivate employees and create a positive working environment.

8. Conduct exit interviews: Management should understand why employees leave and use their feedback to improve retention strategies.

Conclusion

Through this Employee Attrition Analysis, I understood that employee turnover is an important issue that can affect an organisation's performance and productivity. The analysis identified higher attrition among Sales Representatives, employees working overtime, younger employees and those with poor work-life balance.

I believe the organisation can improve employee retention by providing a supportive working environment, better career opportunities, fair workload distribution and employee recognition. Regularly monitoring employee attrition through the Power BI dashboard can also help management make better decisions.
